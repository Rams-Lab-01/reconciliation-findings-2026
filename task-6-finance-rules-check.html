Task 6 — Verification of the AI briefing memo

Subject of review: an internal AI-generated briefing memo to principals on UAE finance-ops rules. This page records what is correct, what is wrong, what is misleading by omission, and which source backs each finding. All sources are official UAE government domains. Each verdict was verified against tax.gov.ae, u.ae, mof.gov.ae and the CBUAE cheques FAQ before being marked.
Summary. Of the 12 statements in the memo, 8 are wrong, 2 are correct, and 3 are correct but incomplete in a way that matters for a DMCC family office. Two verdicts have been corrected relative to an earlier answer: #4 (late VAT penalty AED 10,000, not AED 20,000 — the figure was reduced by Cabinet Decision No. 49 of 2021) and #8 (the cheque-bouncing statement is correct under Federal Decree-Law No. 14 of 2020 and Article 648(2) of Federal Decree-Law No. 50 of 2022). Statements 5, 9, and 10 are each correct in their core claim but incomplete for the office's specific position.

2 — Three correct-but-incomplete statements that matter for a DMCC free-zone family office

Statement 5 (CT rate). True for a mainland taxable person, but it omits the regime that actually governs this office. A Qualifying Free Zone Person under Cabinet Decision No. 55 of 2023 pays 0% on all Qualifying Income with no ceiling, and 9% on Taxable Income that is not Qualifying Income. Critically, a QFZP is NOT eligible for the 0% band on the first AED 375,000 — that relief is unavailable to it. Qualifying status also depends on the de minimis test: non-qualifying revenue must not exceed the lower of AED 5,000,000 or 5% of total revenue, and breaching it costs the regime for that period and the four following. Reading statement 5 alone, a DMCC entity could assume the AED 375,000 band protects it when it does not.
Statement 10 (records). True, but it omits that the seven years runs from the end of the tax period rather than the transaction date, and more importantly that a Qualifying Free Zone Person must maintain audited financial statements as a condition of the 0% rate, together with transfer pricing documentation for related-party transactions. For a family office with related entities and principal-level dealings, that second obligation is the one that bites.
Statement 9 (WPS for a DMCC entity). WPS for a DMCC entity is administered through DMCC rather than MOHRE. Employees of DMCC member companies are not MOHRE-registered, so the correct compliance route for this office is DMCC’s own WPS registration and monthly salary file. The memo’s 50-employee threshold is wrong either way, but the operative regulator here is DMCC.

3 — Single wrong statement that would cost real money or trouble first

Statement 6 (CT return due within 12 months of financial year end). Under Cabinet Decision No. 75 of 2023 late filing attracts AED 500 for each month or part month for the first twelve months, then AED 1,000 per month thereafter, and unpaid tax attracts 14% per annum for each month or part month. The filing penalty alone is modest; the exposure that matters is the 14% running on unpaid tax from the day after the due date, together with the loss of clean-compliance standing at renewal. Believing the corporate tax return is due in twelve months rather than nine means the office would calmly miss a hard statutory deadline by three full months — and unlike the other errors it produces no warning signal beforehand, because nothing goes wrong until the deadline has already passed. Statement 9 (WPS) runs it close: treating WPS as inapplicable would expose the office to MOHRE penalties and suspended work permits, but the office already runs a compliant DMCC WPS file so that error would be caught by existing practice.
