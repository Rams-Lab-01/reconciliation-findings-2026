Task 5 — Design of the monthly-close automation

Brief: monthly close is currently two days of manual work (statement download → categorise → match invoices → chase missing → create top-up tasks → draft principals summary). Stack: Make.com for orchestration, Claude (API + Cowork + Code) for intelligence, Google Workspace as system of record, Todoist for human task tracking. Below is the proposed pipeline, Mermaid architecture diagram, approval gates, failure modes, security posture, and the verbatim first prompt.

Stack rationale: Make.com for orchestration, not n8n. Make.com is the right layer for this office at its current size: deterministic, retryable, audit-logged, and it stays out of the data plane — nothing customer-confidential flows through it. From step 3 onward a redaction step in Make.com strips account numbers, IBANs and PANs from every payload before the call to Claude, so the model sees transaction lines labelled by account rather than real identifiers. The one exception is the statement PDF at step 2, which carries the account number in its header and is handled contractually rather than technically — see point 5. The pipeline may prepare and reconcile; payment execution and any change to vendor bank details stay human-only (see point 4).

2 — Three human approval gates

GateWhere it sitsWhat the reviewer seesWhat the reviewer approves or rejects
G1Before step 5. Nothing downstream — matching, chasing or reporting — runs until it passes.The Review tab only, not all 74 rows. It shows new vendor strings, every model-assigned category with its confidence, every row above AED 10,000, and at the head a control-tie block giving each statement's closing balance against the parsed total with the variance in a single cell.If that variance cell is anything other than 0.00 the gate cannot be passed. Approval is recorded by name and timestamp, not by a click that leaves no trace.
G2Before anything leaves the building.Each draft beside the charge that triggered it: vendor name, amount, date, card, the recipient address as resolved from the vendor master, and the draft body.Emails are sent by hand from Gmail. No scenario in Make.com holds send permission.
G3Before the memo reaches the principals.The full memo with the control-tie block at its foot and the scope limitations stated.The reviewer signs it before distribution. The memo carries the preparer's name because someone must own the numbers.

There is no payment gate in this pipeline, because the pipeline contains no payment step. That is deliberate and is covered at point 4.

4 — What I would NEVER automate end to end

Two things, and both concern money leaving the account.

First, payment execution. No agent gets banking portal credentials, no scenario holds authority to release a payment file, and no AI output is ever the last thing that happens before funds move. The pipeline may prepare a payment schedule and reconcile it afterwards. A human keys or releases it in the portal under dual authorisation.

Second, any change to payment destination data. Vendor bank details, IBANs and new payee creation are never written by an automation, never updated from an inbound email, and never amended by a model. That field changes only through the out-of-band verification process, by a named person, approved by a second named person.

The reason is asymmetry of consequence. A wrong category is corrected in ten seconds at the next close. A wrong transfer is a request for a recall, not a right to one, and the counterparty decides whether you get the money back. An agent that misreads a transposed amount or lifts an IBAN from a fraudulent email produces a loss that no downstream control can reverse. I have seen the pattern already in this screening: a queued payment to a 22-character IBAN with an inflated amount would have executed cleanly had the process trusted the document in front of it.

Card PANs in full, banking portal credentials and MFA devices, signatory mandates, KYC packs, passport and Emirates ID scans and the principals' personal financial documents never leave. That single call runs on an enterprise API tier with a signed DPA, inputs excluded from training and a stated retention window I have confirmed in writing. How I check a tool's data handling before connecting it: I read the DPA and the sub-processor list, confirm in writing that inputs submitted through the API are excluded from model training and establish the stated retention window, check whether a zero-retention or enterprise tier is available, and then run a synthetic statement with fabricated numbers through the whole pipeline before a single real figure touches it.

6 — Verbatim first prompt to Claude Cowork, step 1

You are building the ingestion stage of our monthly close pipeline. Work in the Google Drive folder /Finance/Close/2026-09/01_Statements/, which contains four PDF bank and card statements for one operating account, ACCT-3510, and three corporate cards (Task 5 covers the full card estate; the Task 4 quarterly extract reviewed only CARD 1346 and CARD 0951). For each PDF, extract every transaction line and return a JSON array. Each object must have exactly these keys: date in YYYY-MM-DD, description as the raw string from the statement with no cleanup or interpretation, account_label taken from the filename, debit as a number or null, credit as a number or null, currency as a three-letter code. A line is either a debit or a credit, never both. Do not merge, split, reword or tidy any description. Do not infer a category. Do not skip lines you find unusual, including reversals, fees, interest and foreign currency entries. Also return a control object for each file containing stated_opening, stated_closing, line_count and page_count, read from the statement itself rather than calculated. Then verify yourself: for each file, compute stated_opening plus total credits minus total debits and compare it to stated_closing. Report the variance for each file as an explicit number. If any variance is not exactly 0.00, stop, do not write any output file, and tell me which file failed, the variance, and the three lines you are least confident you read correctly. Do not adjust any figure to make it balance. If all four reconcile, write the combined result to a new Google Sheet named Close_2026-09_Raw with the columns Date, Description, Account, Debit, Credit, Currency, Source_File, Source_Page, and leave Category and Notes as empty columns. Then print a summary table of the four files showing line count, total debits, total credits and variance. Tell me what you could not read and what you assumed. Never fill a gap with a plausible value.

7 — Honesty question

Most recently, on Task 4 of this screening, I used an agentic spreadsheet tool to build a five-sheet FinOps workbook from the 74-row extract: categorised transactions, a month-by-category summary, an anomaly register, a tested-and-cleared tab and an executive dashboard. It got the hard part right, with all three monthly control totals tying to the fils, and got several softer things wrong. It fabricated two invoice references that appear nowhere in the source data, on the two findings I was recommending for cash recovery. It stated a price increase as 25 percent when 734.50 over 1,836.25 is 40.0 percent. It rounded the headline at-stake figure to 30,761.9 when the correct number is 30,761.86, and on the precision-graded brief that alone would have cost marks. On one rebuild it overlaid two different tables in the dashboard register so the row labels and the data belonged to different tables, and on the next it wrote a number into a cell that should have held a KPI label.

I caught each one the same way: I recomputed all 74 rows myself and tied the category totals, the subtotals and the register independently before accepting any figure, and I re-audited every output after each fix rather than assuming a correction had not broken something else. Two of those defects were introduced by the fix to a previous defect, which is the real lesson: with agentic tools the verification pass is not optional and it is not a one-off.

Beyond this, I run a local stack for day-to-day work: a LiteLLM proxy routing across four model tiers, an OpenHands sandbox for agent execution, and dual memory through Mem0 and Graphiti so context persists across sessions. I use Claude and ChatGPT daily, with Canvas and Artifacts for structured documents and scheduled tasks for recurring research. My working rule is that the AI drafts and I own the number, which is why every figure I have submitted in this screening was recomputed by hand before it went in the box.
